How to use the feelings wheel

  1. Start in the middle. Pick whichever of the six core emotions is closest: sad, mad, scared, joyful, powerful, or peaceful.
  2. Move outward. Each ring gets more specific. “Sad” might turn out to be lonely, and lonely might really be abandoned.
  3. Read the guide. Every feeling on the outer ring opens a short guide: how it tends to feel, where it shows up in your body, what it may be asking of you, and what can help.

Not sure where to start? Type a word into the search box. Some feelings sit in more than one place on the wheel, and seeing where yours lands can tell you something. Drag the wheel to spin it, and tap the center to clear your selection.

What is a feelings wheel?

A feelings wheel is a chart that arranges emotions from general to specific. Most of us reach for the same handful of words, like fine, stressed, upset, and tired. The wheel offers more precise ones. Research on “affect labeling” suggests that putting a feeling into words can take some of its intensity away, and a more exact word makes it easier to work out what you need.

This wheel has 6 core emotions in the center, 24 feelings in the middle ring, and 72 in the outer ring. People use it for journaling, therapy and counseling homework, check-ins with a partner or a team, conversations with kids, and simply answering “how are you?” more honestly.

The six core emotions

Sad
The response to loss, hurt, and disconnection. Sadness slows you down and asks for comfort. On the wheel: hurt, depressed, guilty, lonely.
Mad
The response to a blocked goal, a crossed boundary, or unfairness. Anger is energy for protecting something or changing it. On the wheel: hateful, aggressive, antagonistic, withdrawn.
Scared
The response to threat or uncertainty, whether to your body, your belonging, or your sense of self. On the wheel: rejected, weak, threatened, unsure.
Joyful
Delight and aliveness, alone or shared. On the wheel: excited, creative, sensitive, intimate.
Powerful
Feeling capable, valued, and sure of your footing. On the wheel: faithful, hopeful, proud, respected.
Peaceful
Feeling safe, settled, and connected. On the wheel: thoughtful, intimate, trusting, nurturing.

Hurt

Embarrassed Sad › Hurt
How you might feel
Exposed and self-conscious. Replaying the moment and wishing you could disappear or take it back.
What's going on in your body
Hot face or blushing, averted eyes, a nervous laugh, shrinking posture, or a sudden urge to leave the room.
What this might be a call to be
A call to remember that everyone stumbles in public sometimes, and to treat your own slip the way you would treat a friend's.
Ways to overcome this
Breathe out slowly and let the flush pass; tell the story to someone safe; ask whether anyone will remember this in a month; find the humor when you're ready.

Disappointed Sad › Hurt
How you might feel
Let down and deflated. Something you hoped for didn't happen, and the gap between what you pictured and what is stings.
What's going on in your body
A sinking feeling in the stomach, heavy sighs, drooping shoulders, low energy, or a lump in the throat.
What this might be a call to be
A call to honor what you were hoping for, because disappointment points at something that matters to you, and to adjust expectations without abandoning the hope underneath.
Ways to overcome this
Name exactly what you expected; let yourself be sad about it before trying to fix it; separate what you can still influence from what you can't; if a person is involved, tell them plainly and without blame.

How you might feel
Small and wounded. As if someone's words or actions revealed something wrong with you, and you want to hide.
What's going on in your body
Head down, eyes lowered, heat in the face or chest, a caved-in posture, or going quiet and still.

Depressed

Empty Sad › Depressed
How you might feel
Hollow and flat. Not sad exactly, just nothing. Things that used to matter feel distant or gray.
What's going on in your body
Heaviness in the limbs, fatigue that sleep doesn't fix, changes in appetite, a blank stare, or moving on autopilot.
What this might be a call to be
A call to take the low seriously and let yourself be cared for. Emptiness is often exhaustion, grief, or depression asking for attention, not a verdict on your life.
Ways to overcome this
Keep the basics going: food, water, daylight, sleep; do one small thing that used to help, without expecting to enjoy it yet; tell one person how flat things feel; if it lasts more than two weeks, or you have thoughts of not being here, contact a doctor, therapist, or crisis line.

Inferior Sad › Depressed
How you might feel
Less than. Everyone else seems more capable, more attractive, or further along, and you keep measuring yourself against them and losing.
What's going on in your body
Slumped shoulders, avoiding eye contact, a tight stomach around certain people, a quiet voice, or shrinking to take up less space.

Powerless Sad › Depressed
How you might feel

What's going on in your body

Guilty

Remorseful Sad › Guilty
How you might feel
Deep regret. You wish you could undo what you did, and you keep seeing its effect on someone else.
What's going on in your body
An ache in the chest, a knot in the stomach, trouble sleeping, replaying the scene, or tearfulness.
What this might be a call to be
A call to repair. Remorse is conscience working as intended, pointing you toward making it right.
Ways to overcome this
Offer a specific apology with no 'but'; ask what would help, then do it; decide what you'll do differently and write it down; once you've made amends, allow yourself to be forgiven.

Ashamed Sad › Guilty
How you might feel
Not just 'I did something bad' but 'I am bad.' The mistake feels like proof of who you really are.
What's going on in your body
Wanting to hide, heat in the face, avoiding people's eyes, a sick feeling in the stomach, or harsh self-talk on a loop.
What this might be a call to be
A call to move from shame back to guilt, from 'I am wrong' to 'I did wrong,' because only the second can be repaired.
Ways to overcome this
Name the specific action, not your whole character; make amends where you can; speak to yourself as you would to someone you love who made the same mistake; share it with one trusted person or a counselor.

Responsible Sad › Guilty
How you might feel

What's going on in your body

Lonely

Abandoned Sad › Lonely
How you might feel
Left behind. Someone who should have stayed didn't, and their absence feels like an ache or a verdict.
What's going on in your body
A hollow chest, tearfulness, panic or clinginess when people leave, trouble sleeping, or reaching for the phone again and again.
What this might be a call to be
A call to grieve who left, and to turn toward the people who are still here, including yourself.
Ways to overcome this
Let yourself mourn without rushing; reach out to one steady person today; keep small promises to yourself to rebuild trust; if old abandonment keeps replaying in new relationships, a therapist can help untangle it.

Victimized Sad › Lonely
How you might feel
Wronged and unprotected. Something unfair was done to you, and it may feel like no one saw it or stood up for you.
What's going on in your body
A tense, guarded body, jumpiness, stomach upset, tears or anger close to the surface, or exhaustion.
What this might be a call to be
A call to have your experience believed and your safety restored. And, in time, to reclaim your own sense of agency.
Ways to overcome this
Tell someone you trust what happened; write down the facts; if you were harmed or are unsafe, contact appropriate support, such as a counselor, an advocate, HR, or the authorities; notice the choices you do still have, however small.

Fragile Sad › Lonely
How you might feel
Breakable. Like one more hard thing could crack you, and you need the world to be gentle right now.
What's going on in your body
Tears close to the surface, sensitivity to noise or tone of voice, fatigue, shakiness, or a lump in the throat.

Hateful

Critical Mad › Hateful
How you might feel
Sharp and fault-finding. You see exactly what is wrong with someone, and contempt has started to color the list.
What's going on in your body
Narrowed eyes, tight lips, a clenched jaw, a hard tone of voice, or tension across the forehead.
What this might be a call to be
A call to find the standard or value underneath the criticism, and to ask for what you want instead of attacking what you don't.
Ways to overcome this
Turn the complaint into a request; name one thing the person does right; ask whether you're this harsh with yourself too; pause before saying or sending the cutting thing.

Show on the wheel

Dismissive Mad › Hateful
How you might feel
Cold and done. The person or idea seems beneath your attention, not worth the energy of a real response.
What's going on in your body
Eye-rolling, turning away, a flat or sarcastic tone, crossed arms, or a short exhale through the nose.
What this might be a call to be
A call to notice what you're refusing to take in. Contempt often guards a hurt or a fear of being wrong.
Ways to overcome this
Ask one genuine question before deciding; name the hurt beneath the contempt; if distance truly is needed, set a boundary directly instead of writing the person off; remember a time you were dismissed and how it landed.

Show on the wheel

Skeptical Mad › Hateful
How you might feel
Distrustful and unconvinced. You suspect a hidden motive and you're braced against being fooled.
What's going on in your body
A raised eyebrow, crossed arms, leaning back, a tight jaw, or scanning for the catch.
What this might be a call to be
A call to protect yourself wisely, to check facts and let trust be earned in steps, without deciding in advance that everyone is lying.
Ways to overcome this
Ask for evidence rather than assuming; separate this person from whoever burned you before; extend a small test of trust and see what happens; notice when doubt has become a wall rather than a filter.

Aggressive

Provoked Mad › Aggressive
How you might feel
Pushed. Someone hit a nerve, maybe on purpose, and everything in you wants to hit back.
What's going on in your body
A rush of heat, a pounding heart, clenched fists, a tight chest, or words rising fast to your mouth.
What this might be a call to be
A call to choose your response rather than hand it to the person who poked you.
Ways to overcome this
Take one slow breath out before speaking; step away for ten minutes if you can; name exactly what got to you and why; respond later, when you can say it without wanting to wound.

Show on the wheel

Hostile Mad › Aggressive
How you might feel
Ready for a fight. Other people look like opponents, and you're leaning toward attack in words or actions.
What's going on in your body
Tense muscles, a flushed face, a loud or clipped voice, glaring, pacing, or an urge to throw or strike something.
What this might be a call to be
A call to get safe and get calm first, and then to find the threat or hurt that the hostility is defending.
Ways to overcome this
Leave the situation before it escalates; burn off the energy physically with a fast walk, a run, or a push against a wall; don't drive, text, or make decisions while flooded; if hostility is frequent, or frightens you or others, seek anger-management support.

Frustrated Mad › Aggressive
How you might feel
Blocked. You keep trying and it keeps not working, and your patience is wearing thin.
What's going on in your body
Sighing, clenched teeth, tight shoulders, restless hands, a headache building, or an urge to quit or slam something.
What this might be a call to be
A call to change the approach, not just push harder. Frustration marks the gap between effort and result.
Ways to overcome this
Step away and come back fresh; name the exact obstacle; ask for help or a second pair of eyes; shrink the goal to the next doable step; check whether you're tired, hungry, or rushed.

Antagonistic

Hateful Mad › Antagonistic
How you might feel
Consumed by intense dislike. You want the other person to lose, hurt, or disappear, and it takes up a lot of room inside you.
What's going on in your body
A burning chest or stomach, a clenched jaw, tight fists, trouble sleeping, or replaying the same scenes.
What this might be a call to be
A call to tend the wound beneath the hate, which usually grows from deep hurt, fear, or injustice, and to protect yourself without being poisoned by it.
Ways to overcome this
Name what was done and what it cost you; put distance between you and the person if needed; move the energy through exercise or an unsent letter; talk it through with a counselor if it won't loosen; never act on urges to harm someone, and reach out for help instead.

Self-righteous Mad › Antagonistic
How you might feel
Certain and superior. You're right, they're wrong, and you feel justified in letting them know it.
What's going on in your body

Irritated Mad › Antagonistic

How you might feel

Common questions

Why does the same word appear more than once?

A feeling can grow from different roots. Feeling ashamed after being hurt is not the same as feeling ashamed after hurting someone, and hope that comes from excitement differs from hope that comes from trust. Each guide is written for its place on the wheel.

Are some feelings good and others bad?

No. Some are pleasant and some are painful, but all of them carry information. For painful feelings the guides suggest ways to move through them; for pleasant ones, ways to nurture them.

Can I change how the wheel looks?

Yes. The Colors button lets you pick a color scheme or fine-tune individual colors, and gives you a link that reproduces your version.

Is this a substitute for therapy?

No. It is a vocabulary tool and a starting point for reflection. If a feeling is intense, constant, or getting in the way of daily life, please talk with a doctor or a licensed mental-health professional.

Feel4me is an educational tool, not medical or mental-health advice. If you are in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency services or a crisis line. In the US, call or text 988.
